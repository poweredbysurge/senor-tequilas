# What Is Birria Ramen? The Story Behind Germantown's Favorite Bowl

Birria ramen is a bowl of ramen noodles served in birria consommé, the red chile broth from the Mexican braised-meat stew called birria, topped with the shredded braised meat and the usual birria garnishes: onion, cilantro, radish and lime. It is a Mexican and Japanese mash-up, usually traced to a Mexico City menu around 2015, that spread through Los Angeles taco spots and social media. It has been on the menu at Señor Tequila's in Germantown, Maryland since 2021. Below: where it came from, how it differs from birria tacos, how to eat it without making a mess, and what is in our bowl.

## What Is Birria Ramen, Exactly?

Start with birria. Birria is a slow-braised meat stew from the state of Jalisco in western Mexico, traditionally made with goat and now very often with beef. The meat is marinated in dried chiles, garlic and spices, then cooked for hours until it falls apart. The liquid it cooks in is the consommé, and in most birria restaurants the consommé is the point: you dip the taco in it, you drink it from a cup, you argue about whose is better.

Birria ramen takes that consommé and treats it like ramen broth. Cooked wheat noodles go in the bowl, hot consommé goes over them, the shredded birria goes on top, and the garnishes finish it. Some kitchens add a soft-boiled egg or cheese. Most keep it closer to the taco stand. The result eats like ramen, tastes like birria, and is heavier than either.

## Where Birria Ramen Came From

The short, honest answer is that nobody owns the dish, but there is a widely repeated origin. The most common account credits chef Antonio de Livier, who put a bowl called "birriamen" (birria plus ramen) on the menu of his Mexico City restaurant around 2015. The idea traveled north with the quesabirria boom. Quesabirria itself, the cheese-filled birria taco, came out of Tijuana and took over Los Angeles in the late 2010s. By around 2019, birria trucks and restaurants in Los Angeles were ladling the same consommé over noodles. From there it did what food does now: it went on TikTok and Instagram, where a slow pour of red broth over a pile of noodles is about the most watchable thing a kitchen can film.

None of that history is very old, which is part of the appeal. Birria ramen is not a tradition. It is a very good idea that a lot of cooks had at roughly the same time.

## Birria vs Birria Ramen vs Birria Tacos

People search "birria vs birria ramen" and the answer is simpler than it sounds. Same meat, same braise, same consommé. The difference is the vehicle.

- **Birria** is the stew: meat in its consommé, usually with tortillas on the side.

- **Birria tacos** and **quesabirria** put the meat in a tortilla (with melted cheese for quesabirria), usually griddled in the braising fat, with consommé in a cup for dipping. Ours are on the [birria tacos page](/birria-tacos).

- **Birria ramen** puts the meat and the consommé over noodles, so the broth is in every bite instead of on the side.

If you like dipping, order the tacos. If you like soup, order the ramen. Or order one of each for the table and pass them around.

## How to Eat Birria Ramen

There is no wrong way, but there is a better way.

- **Eat it hot and eat it soon.** Ramen noodles keep absorbing broth. Ten minutes of conversation first and the bowl is a different, softer dish.

- **Squeeze the lime early.** The consommé is rich. Acid wakes it up, and the lime is there for that reason, not decoration.

- **Taste before you spice.** Chile heat builds as you eat. At Señor Tequila's the bowl arrives mild, and you ask for salsa diabla if you want more.

- **Spoon and fork, or chopsticks and spoon.** Noodles first with the fork or chopsticks, then the meat, then drink the consommé with the spoon. Or straight from the bowl. Nobody is watching.

- **Add the radish and onion as you go** rather than stirring them in, so they stay crunchy against the soft noodles.

- **Taking it to go?** Keep the consommé separate until you sit down, then pour. That is the whole trick to birria ramen that travels.

## Birria Ramen FAQ

**What is birria ramen made of?**

Ramen noodles in birria consommé, topped with shredded braised birria meat and garnishes. At Señor Tequila's that means Japanese ramen noodles, beef braised eight hours with guajillo, chile de árbol, cloves and garlic, corn, radish, onion, cilantro, consommé and lime.

**Is birria ramen spicy?**

Usually mild to medium, because the chiles in a birria braise are chosen for depth rather than heat. Ours is mild as served. Ask for salsa diabla to make it hotter.

**What is the difference between birria and birria ramen?**

Birria is the braised meat stew with its consommé. Birria ramen is that stew served over ramen noodles, so the consommé becomes the soup instead of a dip on the side. Same meat, same broth, different bowl.

**How do you eat birria ramen?**

Hot and soon. Squeeze the lime first, taste before adding salsa, eat the noodles and meat with a fork or chopsticks, and finish the consommé with a spoon or straight from the bowl. Keep the broth separate until you are ready if it is takeout.
