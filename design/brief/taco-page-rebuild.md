## Rebuild `/taco-tuesday`. The page is currently pointed at the wrong thing.

Three problems with the page as it stands, and they compound:

The hero sells bingo. The eyebrow reads "PLUS FREE BINGO AT 8" and the deck ends on bingo. Bingo is a real thing that happens, but it is not why anyone should eat these tacos, and it is the first thing the page says.

The section headed "Every Night of the Week" contains three taco dishes. The heading promises a weekly calendar and delivers al pastor, perrones and cinco mamalones. One of those two things is wrong.

The section headed "Why Our Tacos" does not mention tacos. Its four bullets are: dine-in areas, free bingo, weekly specials, and that Tuesdays run a wait. Those are logistics. The page asks the right question in a heading and then answers a different one.

### The strategy, so the rewrite has a spine

This restaurant does not run a Taco Tuesday discount and is not going to. The page was built to chase "taco tuesday near me", which is 17,000 searches a month at a keyword difficulty of 46, the hardest term in the whole set because national chains with real deals own it. A visitor arriving from that search expecting a discount and finding none leaves immediately, which costs the ranking anyway.

Meanwhile "tacos near me" is 556,000 searches a month at a difficulty of **zero**, and "best tacos near me" is 73,000 at **zero**. "Authentic mexican food near me" is 7,600 at zero. Those are the terms this restaurant can actually win, they are thirty times larger, and they match what the place genuinely is.

So the page stops competing on a deal it does not have and starts competing on the tacos themselves. The positioning line, which is the client's own and it is a good one: **every day is Taco Tuesday when the tortillas are pressed that morning.** That answers the Taco Tuesday searcher honestly instead of baiting them, and it aims the page at the terms worth having.

### Two things I am explicitly not putting on this page

**Do not write "the only authentically Mexican owned restaurant in Montgomery County"** or any variation of it. It is an unverifiable superlative about competitors, Montgomery County certainly has other Mexican-owned restaurants, and a comparative claim like that is the kind a competitor can challenge. It is also weaker copy than the truth: every restaurant claims authenticity, and almost none of them will tell you their tortillas are pressed at six in the morning. Specifics beat superlatives. The family line in section 2 carries the same idea and is entirely defensible.

**Do not write "smoked meats"** unless someone confirms something is actually smoked. Birria is braised and al pastor cooks on a vertical trompo, and neither is smoking. If there is a smoker in that kitchen I will happily put it on the page, but I am not claiming it on a guess.
